Why certification matters

Electrical work in the home is covered by an electrical safety law

Part P of the Building Regulations was introduced on 1 January 2005. In general, it expects most electrical work in the home to be carried out by an electrician registered with an approved scheme, who then provides you with the right paperwork.

As a NICEIC Domestic installer, we issue the appropriate certification for all of our work and notify local building control in accordance with Part P.

  • P

    Part P

    Part P of the Building Regulations covers electrical safety in homes in England and Wales. Most electrical work in the home has to comply, but not every small job needs to be notified.

  • BC

    Building control

    Notifiable work needs to be told to local building control. A registered installer can do this through their scheme, which is part of what registration is for.

  • EIC

    Certificates

    You should receive paperwork showing that the work was designed, installed and tested to the wiring regulations (BS 7671).

Which certificate will I get?

It depends on the job. Typically, new circuits and larger alterations come with an Electrical Installation Certificate, smaller additions with a Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate, and a periodic inspection with an Electrical Installation Condition Report. After notifiable work you should also receive confirmation that it has been notified to building control.

Why keep the paperwork?

It is evidence that the work was done and tested properly. Solicitors, insurers and future electricians may ask to see it, for example when a property is sold.

Is every electrical job notifiable?

No. Some minor work is not notifiable, and the rules depend on the type of work and the room. Ask us and we will tell you what applies to your job.
